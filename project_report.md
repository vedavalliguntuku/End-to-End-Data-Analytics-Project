# Retail Sales Analytics: Project Report

**Author:** Vedavalli Guntuku · **Tools:** Python, SQL, Power BI · **Period analysed:** Jan 2022 – Dec 2023

---

## 1. Executive summary

A retail business sold ₹245.5M of products across 5,980 orders and 791 customers over two years, earning ₹33.8M of profit
(13.8% margin). Revenue grew 26% in 2023. The analysis found three things that matter for decisions:

1. **Deep discounts destroy profit.** Order lines discounted more than 20% have a *negative* margin (−11.5%).
   Discounts of 11–20% earn only 2.4%, against 18.1% on undiscounted sales.
2. **Laptops dominate revenue but earn little.** They make up 44% of revenue at a 9.6% margin, the lowest in the catalogue.
3. **Revenue is concentrated in a few customers and a few months.** The top 25% of customers generate 65% of
   revenue, and October–November alone bring in 25% of annual sales.

## 2. Business questions

- How are revenue and profit trending, and when is the business seasonal?
- Which categories, products and regions drive profit, and which lose money?
- Does discounting help or hurt?
- How valuable and how loyal are our customers?

## 3. Data

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Source | Synthetic retail dataset generated by `scripts/00_generate_data.py` (seed 42), modelled on a typical store with realistic data quality problems |
| Raw size | 8,986 rows × 17 columns |
| Clean size | 8,810 order lines × 28 columns (5,980 orders, 791 customers, 22 products) |
| Period | 1 Jan 2022 – 31 Dec 2023 |
| Currency | Indian rupees (₹) |

## 4. Method

**Python (loading and cleaning)**: removed 120 duplicate rows; standardised casing and whitespace; parsed three mixed
date formats into one; filled missing customer name, city, payment method and discount with documented defaults;
dropped 56 rows with missing or invalid quantity; corrected 8 price outliers using the product median; created revenue,
cost, profit, margin and date features. Output: a star schema (`fact_orders`, `dim_customers`, `dim_products`).

**SQL**: loaded the star schema into a relational database and wrote 15 queries using joins, CTEs, subqueries,
`CASE`, `RANK`, `LAG`, `NTILE`, running totals and moving averages.

**Power BI**: three-page interactive dashboard on the same data model, with DAX measures for KPIs, year-over-year growth
and a 3-month moving average.

## 5. Findings

### 5.1 Overall performance
| Metric | Value |
|--------|------:|
| Revenue | ₹245.5M |
| Profit | ₹33.8M |
| Profit margin | 13.8% |
| Orders | 5,980 |
| Customers | 791 |
| Average order value | ₹41,050 |

Revenue grew from ₹108.6M in 2022 to ₹136.8M in 2023 (**+25.9%**), and profit grew from ₹15.1M to ₹18.8M.

### 5.2 Seasonality
October and November contribute **25.1%** of revenue (12.2% + 12.9%), driven by festive-season demand. February and
June are the weakest months (5.1% and 5.5%).

### 5.3 Products and categories
| Category | Revenue | Margin |
|----------|--------:|-------:|
| Technology | ₹191.2M | 12.0% |
| Furniture | ₹52.2M | 19.0% |
| Office Supplies | ₹2.0M | 45.2% |

Laptops alone are ₹108.4M (44% of all revenue) at a 9.6% margin. Office supplies earn the best margins but are
under 1% of revenue.

### 5.4 Discounting
| Discount band | Margin |
|---------------|-------:|
| None | 18.1% |
| 1–10% | 13.2% |
| 11–20% | 2.4% |
| 21–30% | −11.5% |

There were 450 loss-making order lines costing ₹1.98M in total. Technology products discounted by 20–30% account for
most of that loss.

### 5.5 Regions
West (34.1%) and South (34.0%) generate 68% of revenue; North is 21.1% and East only 10.8%. Odisha has the lowest
margin (11.4%). Most states earn similar margins (about 13–16%), so the gap is mainly volume, not pricing.

### 5.6 Customers
- The top 25% of customers (Gold) produce **65%** of revenue and place 16 orders on average.
- 591 of 791 customers (75%) placed 4 or more orders, worth ₹382K each versus ₹33K for one-time buyers
  (about 11× more).
- The three segments (Consumer, Corporate, Home Office) perform alike at about a 13.7–13.9% margin, but Home Office has the
  highest revenue per customer (₹341K).

## 6. Recommendations

1. **Cap discounts at 15–20%** and require approval above that, especially on Technology products. Discounts of 20% and
   30% cause almost all of the ₹1.98M of line-level losses.
2. **Protect laptop margins**: review supplier cost and price, and bundle higher-margin accessories (34.9% margin).
3. **Plan for October–November**: build inventory and staffing ahead of the peak, and use quiet months (February, June)
   for targeted promotions.
4. **Reward the top 25%** with a loyalty programme and early access; they carry two-thirds of revenue.
5. **Grow the East region**: only 10.8% of revenue today despite similar margins.

## 7. Limitations and next steps

- The data is synthetic, so the patterns illustrate the method; swap in real data to get real answers.
- No customer acquisition cost or return data, so profit is product-level only.
- Next: forecast monthly revenue (e.g. Prophet or ARIMA) and build an RFM customer segmentation in Python.
